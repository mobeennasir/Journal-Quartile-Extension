Journal Quartile

Phase 2 — quartile and Impact Factor for the year an article was published. JCR data comes from a file you import; SJR is only a labelled fallback.

Single article — look it up one at a time

Pick how you want to find the article. All three ways end with the same result: the journal's quartile and Impact Factor for the article's own publication year.

Paste a DOI, for example 10.1038/nature14539. The extension looks the article up in Crossref.

Paste any publisher article link, for example a nature.com or ScienceDirect link. The DOI is read from the article page itself.

Already on the article page? “Check this page” reads the DOI from the open tab — no page fetch, so a publisher bot check can't block it. Use it when a link is blocked.

For old articles with no DOI or link. Type the title, journal and year and the extension searches Crossref for the article.

Upload a list of articles (Excel / CSV)

Upload a .csv or .xlsx file with one article per row. Columns it understands: title, journal, year, DOI if known, ISSN if known, volume/pages. A missing DOI is fine — each article is found by its title and journal. Returns one result row per article, with the quartile and Impact Factor for that article's own year. You can download the results as a CSV.

Import JCR data (for quartile + Impact Factor)

Upload your JCR export file (CSV or Excel). JCR is subscription-only, so the extension cannot fetch it itself — your uploaded file is the source. It is saved in the extension and reused for every lookup until the next JCR release (about June). One file per JCR edition; you can import several editions. Do not upload anyone else's login — only the export file.

The year the numbers describe — not the year you downloaded it. A 2021 article needs the export that reports 2021 data.
